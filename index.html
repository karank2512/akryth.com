akryth is the layer between an ai product and the people it cannot afford to miss. it reads the whole conversation, checks what the assistant is about to say, and prepares a brief for a human to act on. the brief cannot be sent. that is the point. try the demo.

how it works

  1. 01 / 04

    read.

    in place of the latest message

    the whole conversation, in order, in context. “i’m fine” after three hard days is not the same sentence it was before them.

    the demo screens each turn against the history around it. it is a reading, not a diagnosis.

  2. 02 / 04

    screen.

    in place of the reply

    the assistant’s draft, checked before the person sees it. a draft that claims it contacted, notified or reported someone is never shown.

    models love to say they did something. the claim is the failure mode we hold hardest.

  3. 03 / 04

    hold.

    in place of the report

    a short factual brief opens for a reviewer: what was said, why it was flagged, nothing else.

    there is no send path. not a policy, a missing capability: the code that would send it does not exist.

  4. 04 / 04

    decide.

    in place of the software

    a person acknowledges the case, decides what happens next, and closes it.

    the layer prepares facts. it does not score people, and it never decides for them.

how it works

the hard part

spotting a bad word is the easy part.

the hard part is a real disclosure inside an ordinary hard week: read against the whole conversation, with the uncertainty left on the page where a person can see it.

harder still is acting on it without overreaching: a brief a human can use, written by a layer that cannot send it.

why now

the assistants are already in these conversations.

people already tell ai assistants things they tell few others, and companion ai is already being sued over what it said back. products handle this one of two ways: ignore it, or treat every flagged word as an alarm. both fail the person in the middle.

akryth is the third option: understand first, then let a human decide.

what it is not

akryth is a layer, not a service.

  • not a crisis line. it does not answer anyone in need.
  • not a diagnosis. it screens text in a conversation. it says nothing about a person.
  • not a reporter. there is no send path in the contract. nothing it prepares can leave.

where it stands

a working layer, early.

what exists: a contract two endpoints implement (screen user turns, screen assistant drafts), a reference service that opens and closes review cases, an encrypted evidence store, and the demo on this site. the screening runs on a real model over fictional conversations.

what is missing is everything a real deployment needs: durable storage, tenant isolation, real auth, the consent language a host shows its users, and clinical and legal review. it has not been validated and it is not a product anyone can use yet.
